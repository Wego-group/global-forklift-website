body:
  en:
    - As warehouses add more electric trucks, compact stackers, and specialized
      handling equipment, many managers discover that throughput problems do not
      always come from insufficient lifting capacity. Delays often appear
      because operators switch between different controls, batteries, turning
      behaviors, and maintenance routines during the same shift. Recent
      fleet-upgrade discussions across the industry show that buyers are now
      paying closer attention to standardization. For WEGO customers, that means
      evaluating whether each additional truck type truly solves a handling
      problem or simply adds another layer of training and service complexity.
    - The operating scene should be broken into repeatable roles before the
      fleet is expanded. A site may need one truck for receiving, another for
      narrow-aisle replenishment, and a third for yard transfer, but those roles
      should be defined by load profile and travel pattern, not by habit. Buyers
      should map where congestion forms, which routes are repeated most often,
      and where operators lose time changing equipment or waiting for a charger.
      Throughput improves when truck roles are clear, overlap is limited, and
      each piece of equipment supports a stable part of the warehouse flow.
    - Configuration planning should compare mast heights, residual capacity,
      battery family, charger compatibility, display layout, and wear parts
      across the proposed fleet. If every truck uses a different connector,
      wheel specification, or control logic, the warehouse gains flexibility on
      paper but loses speed in real operation. A more disciplined approach is to
      standardize wherever possible and keep differences only where the site
      genuinely needs them, such as a special attachment, a tighter turning
      radius, or a different outdoor tyre option for a separate yard task.
    - Before quotation, confirm the number of truck roles, average shift
      distance, pallet dimensions, rack clearances, charger count, spare-parts
      preference, operator experience, and whether the site wants one unified
      electric platform or a mixed fleet with separate duties. Buyers should
      also decide how much service simplicity matters compared with maximum
      specialization. These answers help WEGO recommend a fleet structure that
      supports warehouse rhythm instead of creating hidden friction between
      trucks that were bought one by one without a long-term plan.
    - For export projects, WEGO should align manuals, charger families, key
      replacement parts, operator handover, and maintenance intervals before the
      first shipment leaves. Fleet standardization is valuable because it
      reduces downtime after delivery as much as it improves movement inside the
      warehouse. When buyers plan truck commonality early, they usually gain
      faster onboarding, simpler preventive service, and more reliable daily
      throughput without chasing a larger fleet than the site actually needs.
