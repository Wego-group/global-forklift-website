  en:
    - Recent warehouse energy projects are changing how buyers think about electric forklifts. The question is no longer only whether to choose lead-acid or lithium. Many sites now want truck charging to fit a broader power strategy that includes rooftop solar, time-of-use tariffs, backup capacity, and closer visibility into electricity peaks. For WEGO buyers, this means the forklift itself may be only one part of the decision. The charging window, charger output, and number of trucks drawing power at the same time can shape the project more than the nominal battery voltage on a catalog sheet.
    - The operating scene should be mapped in hours, not assumptions. A warehouse with two steady shifts and a lunch break may support opportunity charging very differently from a facility that loads trucks in short, aggressive peaks and then sits quiet for several hours. Some buyers have daytime solar generation but the heaviest outbound work happens after sunset. Others can charge during off-peak hours but need trucks ready before a cold-store team starts. These realities affect whether a lithium package, spare battery strategy, or staged charger layout actually improves uptime.
    - Truck configuration should therefore be linked to battery chemistry, charger count, and parking design from the start. Buyers should confirm battery capacity against daily amp-hour demand, not just nameplate runtime. They should also check cable routes, connector handling, bay ventilation, charger protection from impact, and whether the same charger family can support future fleet expansion. In many warehouse projects, a slightly smaller truck with a disciplined charging routine performs better than a larger truck whose battery plan was left vague until late in the purchase cycle.
    - Before quotation, confirm hours per shift, break timing, truck quantity, charger voltage, local electrical standard, available transformer margin, indoor temperature, and whether charging must happen beside racking, in a separate bay, or near dispatch lanes. Buyers should also decide if the priority is lower energy cost, faster turnaround, less maintenance, or easier scaling for future electric trucks. These checkpoints help WEGO recommend a practical electric forklift package instead of a battery option that looks attractive but creates avoidable infrastructure pressure.
    - For export orders, WEGO should align charger specification, plug format, spare connectors, manual language, startup training, and preventive inspection routines before shipment. A solar-aware forklift project works best when the truck, charger, and facility timetable are planned as one operating system. That preparation reduces charging bottlenecks after arrival and gives the site a clearer path to expand electric handling without rebuilding the whole warehouse power layout again.
