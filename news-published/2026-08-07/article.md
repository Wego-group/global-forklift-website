    - >-
      Recent autonomous forklift and pallet-mover launches have made many warehouse managers revisit the question of where automation really pays back. The market signal is clear: buyers are interested in reducing repetitive travel, labor pressure, and night-shift dependency, but most sites still run a mixed fleet rather than a full automation rebuild. For WEGO buyers, that means the useful comparison is not manual versus autonomous in theory. It is whether a specific route, pallet pattern, and daily schedule are stable enough for automated movement without slowing the rest of the operation.
    - The best starting point is the operating scene, not the technology label. Repetitive line-feeding routes, fixed receiving-to-rack loops, and closed warehouse transfer lanes are usually easier to automate than crowded cross-traffic zones with damaged pallets, irregular overhang, or frequent manual exceptions. If the truck will share space with pedestrians, temporary staging, and visiting trailers, buyers should map intersections, waiting points, scanner locations, and manual override points before asking for a quotation. A stable environment matters more than chasing the newest headline feature.
    - Configuration planning should then focus on truck type, battery routine, sensing clearance, and payload consistency. A walkie stacker or compact electric pallet truck may be enough for repeat indoor transport, while a larger counterbalance truck only makes sense if load size, dock work, or lift height truly requires it. Buyers should also confirm pallet condition, fork-entry direction, floor reflectivity, Wi-Fi or local control coverage, and how the charger is protected during continuous duty. Automation performance depends on these details more than on a generic promise of smart navigation.
    - Before quotation, confirm the exact travel path, stop count per cycle, pallet dimensions, load weight range, door widths, charging window, manual fallback process, and who resets the truck after an alarm or blocked aisle. It is also important to define whether the goal is labor reduction, safer night handling, better repeatability, or simply smoother internal transfer between fixed points. When these priorities are written clearly, the proposed WEGO solution can be matched to the real duty cycle instead of being oversized for marketing language.
    - For export projects, WEGO should align control-language preferences, training expectations, spare wear parts, battery support, and remote troubleshooting responsibilities before shipment. Automation only adds value when the local team can restart, inspect, and maintain the truck without long downtime. A well-planned mixed fleet usually wins over a rushed automation purchase, because it keeps manual flexibility where the site still needs judgment and puts repeatable routes on the equipment best suited for them.
